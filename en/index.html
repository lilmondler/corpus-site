corpus n., pl. corpora — a platform for running a sociological study

/ˈkɔːpəs/ · Lat. body

1.A word processor, spreadsheets, coding software, an online survey tool and a shared folder.

your whole study
in one place,
from consent to quotation

Every quotation leads back to its interview, every interview to the participant’s consent. corpus is a web platform for sociologists that lets you run an entire empirical study in one place: participants and consent, interviews and transcripts, field notes, surveys, networks, coding, quotes and data export to R. Every change is logged, raw data is never overwritten, and every figure and quote keeps its provenance.

§ 01 — the problem

too many programs for one study

An empirical study is usually run across several programs that do not talk to each other: transcripts in a word processor, coding in qualitative analysis software, the survey in an online survey tool, response cleaning in spreadsheets, networks in separate graph software, literature in a reference manager, consent forms in a paper binder. What links them together is file names and the researcher’s memory. This leads to four recurring problems.

  1. Scattered data

    The interview recording on a voice recorder, the transcript in an email, the consent form on paper, the survey answers in a separate spreadsheet.

    In corpus, the interview, its transcript, the survey answers and the consent form are linked to one participant under a code (for example P-014) and open from a single page.

  2. Untraceable versions

    Files called “final”, “final_2”, “edits”. What changed, who changed it and when can no longer be established.

    Every processing step on a transcript — cleaning, anonymisation, translation — creates a new version. The original text is kept, and any two versions can be compared word by word.

  3. Provenance that cannot be recovered

    A supervisor or reviewer asks where the 38% comes from and which questionnaires it was computed on. The answer has to be reconstructed from memory.

    Every exported figure links back to the raw answers and cleaning rules, every quote to the transcript version and line.

  4. Poorly protected personal data

    Participant names in file names, transcripts on a personal laptop or in cloud services hosted outside the EU.

    Participant names and contact details are encrypted and never appear in exports.

§ 02 — what’s inside

modules n. pl.

Screenshots of the platform, version 0.6, on a demo study with fictional data. The qualitative and quantitative data of one study are handled in a single environment. Everything exports to open formats — CSV, JSON, DOCX, R, GraphML — readable without corpus.

  • Topic, problem, question, hypotheses, methodology, fieldwork, analysis, conclusions. Changes to the methodology are saved as versions with a reason.

  • A transcript passage gets a code or becomes a quote, with the participant, text version and line.

  • The “individual — tie type — individual 2” table becomes a graph; density, degree, betweenness and communities are computed.

  • Survey builder, collection by link, cleaning rules with reasons, export to R with a variable dictionary.

  • Which interviews and which participant groups each code appears in.

  • Entry checks catch inconsistencies; a doubtful value becomes a query to whoever entered it.

  • Who changed what and when, the value before and after. Entries are linked in a hash chain.

§ 03 — fieldwork and reflexivity

memo n.

Two things fieldwork keeps producing and that get lost fastest: ideas jotted down along the way and transcripts made on your own computer.

  • § 01COR-MEMO

    Memos and decision log

    Write memos as fieldwork unfolds and keep a record of every methodological choice — what, when and why. Your reflexivity becomes part of the corpus, not a separate file.

  • § 02COR-TRS

    Import your transcripts

    Transcribing with noScribe or Whisper on your computer? Import .srt, .vtt or .json files: corpus keeps the text and the original file — speaker turns are recognised in Whisper JSON — and every correction becomes a new version.

§ 04 — principles

ALCOA+ abbr.

ALCOA+ is the data quality standard used in clinical trials. It requires every record to be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, as well as complete, consistent, enduring and available. corpus applies these requirements to sociological data: for every record you can see who entered or changed it, when and why.

§ 05 — data and security

protection n.

The data of a sociological study almost always includes personal data. Storage and protection requirements are therefore built into the design of the platform.

  • § 01COR-GDPR

    GDPR

    During the pilot, the university or lab will remain the data controller. No real data will be uploaded to corpus before a data processing agreement (GDPR art. 28) setting out each party’s role has been signed.

  • § 02COR-PII

    Participant protection

    Participant names and contact details are encrypted and stored separately from the materials. Consent scans are visible to authorised roles only.

  • § 03COR-WDR

    Withdrawal

    A participant’s withdrawal is recorded in the log; their interviews, transcripts, quotes and answers are excluded from every export. Their entry stays in the register, marked as withdrawn.

  • § 04COR-LOG

    Access and audit trail

    Sign-in with a password and a one-time code sent by email. Roles and permissions are set in each study. Every action is recorded in a hash-chained log.

§ 06 — who it’s for

who it is for pron.

Empirical studies with qualitative, quantitative or mixed data.

  • § 01COR-M1

    Master’s and PhD theses

    A master’s dissertation or a PhD: from topic, problem statement and hypotheses to fieldwork and analysis. The supervisor can follow progress and check where every quote comes from.

  • § 02COR-LAB

    Labs and research teams

    A shared study with roles: lead, researcher, coder, viewer. Coders have no access to participants’ names and contact details in the register; everyone’s actions are in the log.

  • § 03COR-MIX

    Mixed methods

    Interviews, a survey and network data from the same field are linked by the participant code — no manual merging of spreadsheets.

  • § 04COR-EDU

    Teaching research methods

    A demo study with fictional data shows the full cycle, from raw data to a table and a quote. A teaching mode for first-year master’s students is in preparation.

§ 07 — questions

questions n. pl.

Does corpus use artificial intelligence?

No. corpus is not another AI service or a layer on top of ChatGPT: it is a standalone system with its own infrastructure and its own logic. Interpretation always stays with the sociologist.

  • No built-in AI. The platform stores, organises, links and verifies data. It does not code interviews, summarise them or draw conclusions on the researcher’s behalf.
  • Nothing sent to AI services. Interviews, transcripts, surveys and field notes are never sent to any artificial-intelligence service, ChatGPT or otherwise.
  • No model training. Research data is never used to train models. This ban will be written into the data processing agreement and imposed on any technical provider involved in running the platform.
  • In the future: local only. Should assistive features ever be added, they will run on local models, with no data leaving the system. Switched off by default and clearly marked in the interface, they will never alter raw data.
Who is responsible for research data?

During the pilot, the university or lab will remain the data controller. No real data will be uploaded to corpus before a data processing agreement (GDPR art. 28) setting out each party’s role has been signed.

Can I get my data back?

Yes. Everything exports to open formats — CSV, JSON, DOCX, R, GraphML — readable without corpus.

How do I get access?

Access is by invitation. Write to us: we will open a demo study with fictional data and, for the pilot, a dedicated space for your lab.

How much does corpus cost?

corpus is not sold. During the closed pilot, access is by invitation. Terms of use after the pilot have not been set yet; they will be published on this site.

§ 08 — where we are

pilot n.

corpus is designed and developed in Paris by Snezhana Galimova, trained in sociology (bachelor’s degree from Université de Versailles Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines, master’s at Sorbonne Université) and with several years’ experience handling clinical-trial data. An independent project, corpus brings these two practices together: the logic of sociological fieldwork and rigorous data handling.
