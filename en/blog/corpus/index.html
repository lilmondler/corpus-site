corpus, n.

Why the platform is called what it is called, and what the “body of a study” means when it lives in twelve different folders.

date
October 6, 2026
reading
5 min read
author
Snezhana Galimova

corpus [ˈkɔːpəs] n., pl. corpora — Latin for “body”. In linguistics, it is a collection of texts against which hunches about language are tested. In law there is habeas corpus, “you shall have the body”: a demand to produce the person, not an account of them. I like the fact that one word holds both meanings: the collection, and the demand to produce it.

The body of a study

Every empirical study has a body. It is not the text of the thesis or the article; it is everything they are made of: interview recordings, transcripts, field notes, questionnaires, photographs, tables of relationships, scanned consent forms, notes from sources. The body is what remains once the conclusions have been written. And it is what people return to when someone challenges those conclusions.

The problem is that the body of a study is almost never kept in one place. The audio is on the recorder and in the cloud. The transcript is in someone’s email, in three versions. The consent form is in a paper folder, and the folder is in a rucksack. The questionnaires are in Excel, cleaned by hand, and exactly how they were cleaned is remembered only by the person who did it, and only until their holiday. The codes are in a qualitative analysis package, the quotations are in a draft chapter, and the only link left between a quotation and its line in the transcript is memory.

as long as you remember everything, it feels like order.

This is not carelessness. It is how the work goes: fieldwork happens quickly, analysis slowly, and between them there are months, and sometimes other people. The researcher who conducted the interviews and the researcher who codes them are often different people, even when they share a name.

What it means to “produce the body”

In sociology, verifiability is usually understood as transparency of method: describing the sample, the topic guide, the coding procedure. That matters, but it is an account of the body, not the body itself. Real verification is when you can walk back from a conclusion: from a sentence in the article to the quotation, from the quotation to a version of the transcript, from that version to the audio recording, from the recording to the participant’s consent. And when, behind the figure “38% of respondents”, you can see which questionnaires were included, which were excluded and why.

In clinical trials this path back is mandatory and has long been formalised; there is a separate post about that. In sociology it more often exists as a good intention. Not because sociologists are less careful, but because they have no tool in which carefulness is the default behaviour rather than extra work done in the evenings.

Why a dedicated platform

corpus is an attempt to build such a tool. Not yet another coding program, and not a cloud drive full of folders, but a place where the body of a study is kept whole and every part of it has a provenance.

It all rests on a few rules:

  • The source is untouchable. An uploaded file is never overwritten. The first version of a transcript and the raw answer to a questionnaire never change.
  • Processing is a recipe. Cleaning a text or a questionnaire is not an edit on top, but a new version that remembers what it was made from and how. You can revise the recipe, and the result is rebuilt.
  • Every action is recorded. Who, what, when, what it was before and what it became. The entries are linked in a chain, so the past cannot be quietly rewritten.
  • The why is data too. A decision such as “exclude questionnaires completed in under a minute” is recorded together with its reason.
  • People are behind codes. Participants’ names and contact details are encrypted and never appear in any export.

That sounds strict, and it is meant to. But rigour should not feel like bureaucracy. A good notebook does not demand discipline from you; it simply won’t let a page be torn out unnoticed.

What corpus is not

It is not a “smart” system that will analyse your interviews for you. Interpretation remains the researcher’s job, and it is the best part of the job. corpus is there to free up time for it: less searching for files, less comparing versions, less trying to remember where a figure came from.

Nor is it a replacement for R, Python or Word. Data leaves corpus in open formats (CSV, JSON, DOCX, GraphML) and stays readable without it. The body of a study should not depend on the program it is stored in.

Why Latin

We could have used an English word. But “body” in English is also a car body, a governing body, a body of water and the body of a letter. corpus, on the other hand, is at once a collection of texts, a body, and the demand to produce it. Three meanings: the three senses in the dictionary entry on the home page.
